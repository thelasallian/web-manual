## 2.1. Newbie or Probationary Staffers

Newbies are staffers who have recently joined the Web section and have been part of the publication for only one or two terms. As part of standard policy, they are not permitted to publicize their membership in the publication on social media or other platforms until they have successfully undergone the process of regularization.

Five months, or approximately two terms, after their entry, newbies are subjected to the regularization cycle. Failure to meet the requirements of this cycle results in their reclassification as probationary staffers.

Probationary staffers are considered an extension of newbie status. They are required to pass the subsequent regularization cycle to secure full membership. Failure to do so will result in termination from the publication, unless an extension of the probationary period is granted at the discretion of the Section Editor.

In terms of platform access, probationary staffers or newbies are limited to X and the official website. Beginning with TLS64 Term 3, additional access to Telegram may be granted after an appropriate period of service, subject to the decision of the Section Editor. The Editor, however, must ensure that staffers are properly oriented, trained, and capable of managing the responsibilities associated with expanded access.

Web staffers must fulfill the following duties and responsibilities:

1) Monthly event art captions;  
2) Print captions;  
3) Active newsroom participation, including but not limited to: posting, spotting errors, and retrieving links and visuals;  
4) Managerial tasks;  
5) Attendance and participation in org-wide or section-wide events, meetings, and trainings as mandated by the Editor;  
6) Coverages and other assignments;  
7) Social media management;  
8) Website upload;

Apart from these duties, WebDev staffers have the following additional tasks:

1) Website maintenance;  
2) Special projects;  
3) Systems development;  
4) Web Specials  
   

Web Developers also include both developers and designers. Since TLS Web’s WebDev projects are seasonal, they are still expected to do Web-aligned tasks (social media management and caption). Some duties can be omitted or skipped for some months as instructed by the Editor. They shall also be subjected to a monthly evaluation by the Editor.

## 2.2. Regular Staffers

Regular staffers are those who have successfully passed the regularization cycle. They are allowed to publicize their membership and include it in their experience in forms and other formal settings. It is important to note that regularization does not equate to passivity when it comes to work; regulars are expected to serve as role models or mentors to newbies and probationary members.   
Regular staffers can be deregularized or put on probation after failing to fulfill the duties outlined in 2.1. They will also be given access to all social media platforms once they are regularized.

## 2.3. Terminated or Resigned Staffers

Staffers who intend to resign must first complete all pending tasks and assignments before the effectivity of their resignation. Resignations shall take effect one month after the formal filing of the request. Once resignation is finalized, the staffer will be removed from the section’s and the publication’s records and must relinquish all access to TLS social media accounts, the website, and other internal systems.

Should resigned staffers wish to reapply, they shall be subject to the procedures and requirements stipulated in the TLS Bylaws.

Terminated staffers are those who have either repeatedly failed to fulfill their assigned duties or have engaged in grave misconduct, as defined under the TLS Bylaws. Termination results in the permanent revocation of membership in the publication.

In such cases, staffers must surrender access to all TLS-related accounts, platforms, and files. Unlike resigned staffers, terminated staffers are permanently barred from reapplying to **The LaSallian**.

## 2.4. Editorial Board Members

These are Web staffers who are part of the Editorial Board (EB) like the Executive Editors or Managers. EB members are not duty-bound by the tasks enumerated in 2.1. Upon agreement with the Section Editor, they can still contribute. 

## 2.5. Editor at Large

These are senior staffers and previous EB members. Similarly, they are not duty-bound by tasks but can still contribute to the section upon agreement with the Section Editor.

## 2.6. Assistant Web Editor

Assistant Editors (AE) serve as the immediate support to the Web Editor (WE) and function as the latter’s deputies in managing the section. In the absence of the WE, all editorial concerns and operational matters shall be directed to the assistant editor.

The position is filled on a termly basis through an application process that includes an interview and an examination administered by the Section Editor. Should no applicant qualify or express willingness to assume the role, the Section Editor may appoint an AE at their discretion, who can also be a Manager.

AEs are expected to demonstrate leadership, reliability, and familiarity with section operations in order to effectively assist in editorial management and decision-making.

Apart from fulfilling the duties outlined in 2.1, they are expected to accomplish or do the following:

1) Edit captions from staffers before passing them to the Editor;  
2) Approve Instagram stories;  
3) Website preview;  
4) Devising content calendar or schedules;  
5) Section management tasks, including staffer management, conflict resolution, holding meetings, finding staffers to cover and take on assignments, and tracking coverages and assignments;  
6) Take on coverages and assignments without staffers;  
7) Represent the section in the absence of the WE;  
8) Other tasks as directed by the WE

A maximum of two assistant editors can be selected or appointed, depending on the situation and as permitted in the Bylaws, which also dictates who qualifies as an assistant editor.

## 2.7. Web Editor

The WE is entrusted with the leadership of the Web section and is responsible for determining the direction and priorities the section shall pursue, subject to the approval of the Editor in Chief. In this capacity, the WE serves as the principal representative of the section in the EB and acts as the primary decision-maker for all matters concerning Web operations.

The WE shall represent the section in EB functions, ensuring that the perspectives and needs of Web are communicated and considered in publication-wide deliberations. As the head of the section, the Web Editor is ultimately accountable for the performance, discipline, and output of Web staffers.

Specifically, the Web Editor is responsible for the following:

1) Editing captions;  
2) Approving and developing content;  
3) Website preview;  
4) Devising or approving a content calendar;  
5) Web Special project management;  
6) Intersection coordination;  
7) Section management tasks;  
8) Take on coverages and assignments without staffers or available assistant editors;  
9) Monthly staffer grades and evaluations;  
10) Staffer management and development (i.e., training, meetings, including meetings and trainings;  
11) Staffer membership;  
12) Recruitment Week (i.e., exams, interviews, decision of acceptance, catalog, marketing);  
13) Other tasks requiring participation of the section (e.g., Staffer box, events)

Apart from these, the WE shall also hold ownership or administrative control over the following:

1) Facebook  
2) Instagram  
3) Meta  
4) X  
5) Telegram  
6) Website  
7) CPanel  
8) Pingloi and other bots
