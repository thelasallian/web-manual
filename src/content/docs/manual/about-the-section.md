## 1.1. History

In 2003, the website was opened under Editor in Chief (EIC) Isabelle Yujuico but was shortly closed down after being hacked. It was not until 2006, that The LaSallian (TLS) website was relaunched. In 2009, under EIC Gianina Densing our Facebook page was launched. On June 1, 2011, we launched our X (formerly Twitter) account to provide live coverage of President Benigno Aquino III’s visit to DLSU during the Centennial Celebration.

In 2012, under EIC Patrick Ong, TLS won the Award of Excellence for its social media presence. In the succeeding year, TLS also launched its Instagram account. In 2015, our social media broke a 100,000 likes. Following this, the Web section was formalized, with Angelika Tirona serving as the first editor. In 2017, TLS’ X account reached 100,000 followers and got verified, but was removed when X introduced premium subscriptions in 2023.

In 2019 and 2020, the TLS website got hacked. Web Editor (WE) Rafael Gabriel Arceo then launches the revamped website. Two years later, TLS launched its Telegram channel and included itself in Google News under Emmanuelle Castañeda’s term. Succeeding her, Asst. WE Eloisa Limbago now serves as the Web Editor for TLS62. 

Under TLS62, five Web Specials were released: [Archives Special 2022](https://thelasallian.com/archives-special-2022/), [Make-Up Elections 2022](https://mue2022.thelasallian.com/), [Women’s Month Special](https://women.thelasallian.com/), [DLSU Brothers Special](https://brothers.thelasallian.com/), and the [Rant and Rave website](https://rnr.thelasallian.com/) (which was started in TLS61). Additionally, a [preview page](https://thelasallian.com/preview/) was created this year and is continuously being updated. In March, the day before TLS’ annual spoof article release, the spoof website went down but was eventually restored on April Fools’ Day. However, due to several criticisms of some spoof articles, many were taken down from both the website and social media accounts. Sometime this year, Asst. WE Ronn Parcia began drafting the [Newsbites Manual](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1ecOw1OkGJokTwv2kMtNvdzv-HeVu9wY0kXR8VGURSz4/edit?tab=t.0#heading=h.b1djrujltkvb), a compilation of the newsroom rules that Web staffers should know. In the same year, Web Staffer Gleezell Uy developed “Pingloi,” a Telegram bot that allows users to set up tags. By the end of the academic year, Asst. WE Ramon Martinez wins the EB Race and is hailed as TLS63’s Web Editor.  

The Web Special for [Special Elections Special 2023](https://se2023.thelasallian.com/) was released during the first term. Additionally, a [catalog page](https://thelasallian.com/catalog/) was also released to showcase TLS’ best works across its section for recruitment weeks. Internally, the website had faced a number of issues. During Term 2 of this year, 62 WE Eloisa Limbago became the first Associate Editor from the Web section. In March of that same year, Trello announced changes to its free tier, which restricted the number of users a free workspace could have. WE Ramon Martinez, together with 59 & 60 WE Rafael Gabriel Arceo, began the migration process by identifying alternative platforms that could handle TLS’ requirements and use cases. By May, WE Ramon Martinez migrated the whole workspace from Trello to Notion. **TERM 3** During this period, ETM Meijie Ureta also won the Editorial Board (EB) Race 64 to become the first Managing Editor (ME) from Web. The Special EB Race 64 was held as multiple vacant seats were in the EB, in which Asst. WE Clark Siao emerged as the next Web Editor.

In TLS64,  the TLS X account was hacked, but no public announcement was made as the delegation function of TLS was still in effect for the account of 63 WE Ramon Martinez and 63 Externals & Training Manager (ETM) and  ME Meijie Ureta. WE Clark Siao created a “dummy” X account (@tlsweb1960) to serve as the designated account for all Web staffers in the meantime. After continuous emails with X support, WE Clark Siao was able to retrieve the account, reset all passwords, and reinforce better security. After two years, the [Pride Web Special](http://pride2025.thelasallian.com) was also released, followed by a [GE Web Special](http://ge2025.thelasallian.com), then a Vangie Web Special. During this year, 62 Office Manager (OM) Angelo Guerra developed the Automated Pubs for Newsbites and Asst. WE Jabin Guamos developed the bitly shortening bot. The [Sports Manual](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Hvx1EQ_xXETwIrl6bjk2BI0JhIZYcSeq8bZrgoNWEEs/edit?tab=t.0) for Web was also developed this year. During the EB Race 65, Jabin Guamos succeeds the leadership as the next WE.  WE Clark Siao also participates and assumes the role of  EIC, becoming the first EIC from Web.

## 1.3. Description

With the world increasingly mediated through digital platforms, the Web section plays a crucial role in bridging the gap between traditional print journalism and the fast-evolving online landscape. The section is composed of social media managers, copywriters, designers, and developers who collectively ensure that The LaSallian (TLS) remains accessible, relevant, and dynamic in the digital sphere. Their work encompasses the formulation of content strategies, the management of official social media accounts, the design and development of websites, and the streamlining of internal systems through innovative digital projects.

As the backbone of TLS’s online presence, the Web section enables the publication to extend its storytelling beyond the printed page, thereby reaching wider audiences and adapting to the demands of contemporary media consumption. Its primary functions include social media management, which involves curating and scheduling content across multiple platforms; website uploading, which ensures that articles and multimedia outputs are made available to readers in a timely and accurate manner; and copywriting, which requires the drafting of captions and materials derived from the content of other sections.

Given the technical and creative nature of these responsibilities, Web staffers are expected to possess strong digital literacy, quick problem-solving skills, and creative adaptability. Moreover, they must demonstrate proficiency in the English language and maintain strict adherence to **The LaSallian**’s Points of Style to ensure accuracy, consistency, and professionalism across all digital outputs.

At present, TLS maintains its presence through multiple platforms, namely: Facebook, Instagram, X (formerly Twitter), Telegram, Google News, and its official website. The stewardship of these platforms rests primarily on the Web section, whose work sustains the publication’s visibility, accessibility, and credibility in an ever-expanding digital environment.
